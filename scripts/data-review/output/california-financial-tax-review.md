# California financial, public-money and tax offenses: combined publication

28 proposed choices from 10 primary sections; 6 reused dependencies and 27 newly monitored sections. 10 newly monitored sources reuse the research packet. 17 instruction entries receive bounded matches.

- Twenty-eight choices in a combined financial/public-money/tax group, not full family or statewide completeness.
- The personal income/corporation tax provisions do not establish federal or sales-tax offenses. Law-code identity stays explicit.
- Tax willfulness means intentional violation of a known legal duty; ordinary mistake is not silently elevated to willful evasion.
- Express fine-or-imprisonment alternatives are read with PEN 18(b)/17(b), not treated as an invariably felony disposition.
- Adult individual penalties, distinct corporate fine caps, investigation costs and the 364-day misdemeanor ceiling are kept separate.
- English-only additions retain fallback notices. Current acquisition and expiry dates are unchanged.

## Decisions

- Display titles describe source conduct when no short statutory title exists. Exact subdivisions and common citation aliases are retained.
- 115 requires a legally recordable kind of instrument and knowing falsity; procurement/offering does not require actual successful recording or an added intent-to-defraud element. The 18(a) state-prison default is not silently converted to 1170(h).
- 548 excludes fire from casualty and prescribes imprisonment AND a fine; specified prior convictions add separate alleged/proved terms.
- 532a(1)-(3) retain their different making, obtaining and reaffirming acts. The false-identity sentence of (4) is a separate choice with distinct $5,000 and $2,500 fine alternatives, not a second copy of the ordinary penalty sentence.
- 529 retains an additional act after personation and the special intent for instruments in (a)(2). Mere false-name reporting does not automatically establish this offense.
- 424(a)(1),(2),(5),(6),(7) use the knowledge/criminal-negligence rule in retained CALCRIM 2765 and Stark v. Superior Court; (3)/(4) retain their distinct knowledge/fraud requirements. Ordinary negligence and lawful incidental use are not swept in.
- 19701(a) keeps the $15,000 delinquency threshold and follows retained CALCRIM 2800/2810 on two-year repetition for both routes and knowing falsity for false filings. The mental-incapacity exception and nonconclusive evidentiary presumptions remain explicit. Separate recoverable penalties are not collapsed into the criminal fine.
- 19706 requires both willfulness and intent to evade; 19705(a)(1) instead requires a materially false declaration under penalty of perjury. Retained CALCRIM 2811/2812 distinguish these elements using People v. Hagen.
- 19705/19708 expressly authorize a fine OR imprisonment; PEN 18(b) supplies the county-jail alternative and PEN 17(b) the misdemeanor route. This is not inferred from the generic additional fine under 672. Their 1170(h) route differs from the state-prison route in 19706.
- The $200,000 corporate ceiling in 19705(b) is disclosed without assigning an individual a corporate punishment. Other tax and investigation liabilities remain additional.
- 19709 says with or without intent to evade, but retained CALCRIM 2828 and People v. Singer require a willful violation of a known duty. That distinction is retained rather than treating an inadvertent withholding error as criminal.
- Instruction matches are cumulative and bounded. All prior justice/custody and property/arson matches stay intact. No source snapshot or expiration is renewed by this publication.

## Interpretation authorities

- [Stark v. Superior Court (2011) 52 Cal.4th 368](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2011/s145337/): Knowledge or criminal negligence for the specified public-money routes; linked by the retained instruction.
- [People v. Hagen (1998) 19 Cal.4th 652](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/4th/19/652.html): Tax willfulness and known legal duty as incorporated in the retained instructions; not a new litigation-currentness receipt.
- [People v. Singer (1980) 115 Cal.App.3d Supp. 7](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/3d/115/supp7.html): Willfulness for former section 19409, applied to current 19709 by retained CALCRIM 2828; not a claim that the 1980 opinion cites the current number.

## Proposed choices

### Procuring or offering a false or forged instrument for public filing: PEN 115(a)

Knowingly procuring or offering a false or forged instrument for filing, registration or recording in a California public office when a genuine instrument of that kind could lawfully be filed. The offering itself can complete the offense; actual recording or successful fraud is not required. Each instrument and statutory procurement/offer is separately punishable.

Felony: 16 months, 2 years or 3 years in state prison under section 18(a). A discretionary base fine up to $10,000 may apply under section 672. Section 115(c) limits probation for specified repeat or multiple-violation cases, subject to its unusual-case exception. Adult individual base penalties are shown. Restitution, assessments, enhancements, probation and case-specific sentencing require separate review.

Mental state: Knowledge that the qualifying instrument is false or forged.
Dependencies: PEN:17, PEN:18, PEN:672.
[Official statute](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=115.)

### Repeated failure to file, or false tax filings or information: RTC 19701(a)

For covered California income/franchise taxes, repeatedly failing to file required returns or supply information, or repeatedly making or supplying knowingly false or fraudulent filings or information, over at least two years and resulting in estimated delinquent tax liability of at least $15,000. CALCRIM 2800 and 2810 retain the repetition requirement for both routes; intent to evade is not required. Corporate-officer and filing presumptions are not conclusive proof. The criminal punishment sentence excludes people who are mentally incompetent or have dementia, Alzheimer's disease or a similar condition.

Misdemeanor: up to one year of imprisonment, a base fine up to $5,000, or both, plus investigation and prosecution costs. Section 19701 also describes a separately recoverable penalty up to $5,000; the criminal fine is not a total liability cap. Adult individual base penalties are shown. Restitution, assessments, enhancements, probation and case-specific sentencing require separate review. Any stated one-year misdemeanor term is capped at 364 days under Penal Code section 18.5.

Mental state: Intent to evade is not required. The false-filing/information route requires knowledge of falsity under CALCRIM 2810; the duty, repeated conduct and delinquency threshold must be proved.
Dependencies: PEN:17, PEN:18.5, RTC:17001, RTC:18501, RTC:18601, RTC:19702, RTC:19703, RTC:23001.
[Official statute](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=RTC&sectionNum=19701.)
