## ca-assault-on-peace-officer

Assault against a peace officer engaged in the performance of duties, where the person knew or reasonably should have known the protected status and duty circumstances (§241(c)). This record addresses the peace-officer branch; the section also names other protected workers. Officer status, performance of duties, and the assault elements must be established.

Under §241(c), up to 364 days in county jail (§18.5), a base fine up to $2,000, or both. This does not supply the punishment for battery, weapon-based assault, or another protected-victim provision. Assessments and other case-specific consequences require separate review.

## ca-burglary-in-the-first-degree

Burglary under §459 of a dwelling or other inhabited location described in §460(a), with the required intent at entry. Inhabited means currently used for dwelling purposes, whether someone is present or not. An empty home is not automatically second-degree burglary; the actual use and charged location matter.

First-degree burglary is punishable by 2, 4, or 6 years in state prison under §461(a). This is not the second-degree county-jail alternative in §461(b). Enhancements, probation eligibility, fines, restitution, and other consequences require separate review.

## ca-dui-second-offense

A §23152 DUI with one qualifying prior violation resulting in conviction within the 10-year period specified by §23540. Qualifying priors include the specified alcohol/drug-related reckless-driving disposition, not every reckless-driving conviction. Verify the violation dates, convictions, and any prior felony allegation rather than relying on the label “second offense.”

Ordinary §23540 punishment without probation: 90–364 days in county jail and a $390–$1,000 base fine. With probation, §23542 requires the fine and jail under either its 10-day minimum branch or its 96-hour branch (two continuous 48-hour periods); the upper limit is 364 days (§18.5). An 18- or 30-month DUI program is required under the probation provision. Section 23550.5 can instead permit felony punishment for specified priors. License, interlock, assessments, and other consequences require separate review.

## ca-dui-third-offense

A §23152 DUI with two qualifying prior violations resulting in convictions within the 10-year period specified by §23546. The dates and qualifying dispositions must be checked, including any applicable alcohol/drug-related reckless-driving prior. A prior felony can invoke a different sentencing provision.

Ordinary §23546 punishment: 120–364 days in county jail and a $390–$1,000 base fine. Probation normally retains that minimum under §23548(a); a court-approved 30-month treatment option under §23548(b), on specific request and good cause, substitutes a 30-day minimum, with the same 364-day maximum (§18.5). Section 23546 also specifies a three-year habitual-traffic-offender designation. Section 23550.5 may instead permit felony punishment for specified priors. Programs, license revocation, interlock, assessments, and other consequences require separate review.

## ca-grand-theft-agricultural-487-b1a

Theft of the domestic fowls or agricultural products listed in §487(b)(1)(A). Its literal threshold is more than $250, but §490.2 generally treats theft worth $950 or less as petty theft, subject to its prior-conviction exceptions. Do not infer felony exposure from the $250 language alone. This is not the livestock provision in §487a.

If §490.2 requires petty-theft treatment, §490 permits up to 6 months in county jail, a base fine up to $1,000, or both. Otherwise the applicable grand-theft branch in §489(c) permits misdemeanor jail up to 364 days (§18.5) or felony imprisonment under §1170(h), ordinarily 16 months, 2 years, or 3 years. Qualifying priors, custody location, restitution, fines, and enhancements require separate review. The livestock-specific fine in §489(b) must not be transferred to this agricultural-products provision.

## ca-grand-theft-firearm-487-d2

Theft of a firearm under §487(d)(2). Section 490.2(c) expressly excludes firearm theft from its general petty-theft rule for property worth $950 or less. This is a theft allegation, distinct from unlawful possession or carrying of a firearm.

Grand theft of a firearm carries 16 months, 2 years, or 3 years in state prison under §489(a). The non-firearm misdemeanor/county-jail alternatives in §489(c) do not supply this branch’s ordinary punishment. Enhancements, fines, restitution, and other consequences require separate review.
