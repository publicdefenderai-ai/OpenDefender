## ca-unlawful-sexual-intercourse-261-5-b

Intercourse with a nonspouse under 18 whose age is no more than three years older or younger than the accused (§261.5(a)–(b)). The close-in-age rule makes this branch a misdemeanor; it is not a blanket exemption from criminal liability.

Misdemeanor under §261.5(b). The default §19 punishment is up to 6 months in county jail, a base fine up to $1,000, or both. Section 261.5(e) separately permits civil penalties for adults and an additional fine up to $70 based on ability to pay. Civil penalties are not jail terms. Registration, including possible court-ordered registration under §290.006, and other consequences need individual review.

## ca-unlawful-sexual-intercourse-261-5-c

Intercourse with a nonspouse under 18 who is more than three years younger than the accused (§261.5(a), (c)). The more-than-three-year gap differs from the close-in-age misdemeanor and from subdivision (d)’s specific age thresholds.

Under §261.5(c), misdemeanor jail up to 364 days (§18.5) or felony imprisonment under §1170(h), ordinarily 16 months, 2 years, or 3 years. The §1170(h) exclusions determine custody location; this does not guarantee county-jail placement. Section 261.5(e) separately permits civil penalties for adults and an additional fine up to $70 based on ability to pay. Civil penalties are not jail terms. Registration, including possible court-ordered registration under §290.006, and other consequences need individual review.

## ca-unlawful-sexual-intercourse-261-5-d

Intercourse by a person aged 21 or older with a nonspouse under 16 (§261.5(a), (d)). These exact age thresholds matter; this is not a rule covering every adult/minor age difference.

If probation is granted, §261.5(f) bars community service at a school or where children congregate. Section 261.5(e) separately permits civil penalties for adults and an additional fine up to $70 based on ability to pay. Civil penalties are not jail terms. ## ca-gross-vehicular-manslaughter-191-5-a

Unlawful killing without malice while driving in violation of VEH §§23140, 23152, or 23153, with the gross negligence and proximate causation required by PEN §191.5(a). Intoxication and a death alone do not establish all elements; the statute also preserves possible murder prosecution when malice is established.

Ordinarily 4, 6, or 10 years in state prison (§191.5(c)(1)). Specified prior convictions instead trigger 15 years to life under §191.5(d), only when the required facts are alleged and admitted or found true (§191.5(h)); not every DUI prior qualifies. If probation is granted, the current §191.5(e) requires 3–5 years. Enhancements, license consequences, fines, and restitution require separate review.

## ca-vehicular-manslaughter-191-5-b

Unlawful killing without malice while driving in violation of VEH §§23140, 23152, or 23153, without gross negligence but with the unlawful act or manner and proximate causation required by PEN §191.5(b). Distinguish the gross-negligence branch rather than using the DUI label alone.

Under §191.5(c)(2), misdemeanor county jail up to 364 days (§18.5), or felony imprisonment under §1170(h) for 16 months, 2 years, or 4 years. The upper felony term is 4 years, not the default 3. Section 191.5(d)’s 15-to-life prior-conviction provision applies to subdivision (a), not this branch. If probation is granted, §191.5(e) requires 3–5 years. Custody eligibility, enhancements, license consequences, fines, and restitution need separate review.

## ca-sexual-battery-243-4-a

Touching a legally defined intimate part against the person’s will, for sexual arousal, gratification, or abuse, while that person is unlawfully restrained by the accused or an accomplice (§243.4(a)). Subdivision (f) requires skin contact for this branch; subdivision (e)’s broader through-clothing rule must not be substituted.

Under §243.4(a), misdemeanor jail up to 364 days (§18.5) with a fine up to $2,000, or felony state prison for 2, 3, or 4 years with a fine up to $10,000. Section 243.4(k) makes its minor-victim/prior-felony combination felony-only, with the stated prison term and fine. Registration under §290, sentencing factors, enhancements, and other consequences require separate review.

## ca-sexual-battery-243-4-e1

Against-will intimate touching for sexual arousal, gratification, or abuse, including the compelled-touching conduct specified in §243.4(e)(1). Physical contact may be direct or through either person’s clothing (§243.4(e)(2)); unlawful restraint is not an element of this misdemeanor branch.

Misdemeanor under §243.4(e)(1): up to 6 months in county jail, a fine up to $2,000, or both. If the accused was the victim’s employer, the fine ceiling is $3,000 with the same jail limit. This is not subdivision (a)’s felony/state-prison alternative. Registration under §290 and other case-specific consequences require separate review.

## ca-lewd-act-child-288-a

A willful lewd or lascivious act upon or with the body of a child under 14, with the sexual intent required by §288(a). Do not substitute the separate 14-or-15-year-old/ten-year-gap branch, or automatically import subdivision (b)’s force requirement into this record.

Ordinary §288(a) term: 3, 6, or 8 years in state prison. If personal infliction of statutory bodily harm is pleaded and proved, §288(i) instead requires life with the possibility of parole. Section 288(e) permits an additional fine up to $10,000 for this branch. Section 667.61 may supply other life-term exposure for specified allegations; registration under §290, enhancements, and other consequences require separate review.

## ca-lewd-act-child-288-c1

A lewd act with the sexual intent specified in §288(a), involving a child aged 14 or 15 and a person at least ten years older (§288(c)(1)). The ten-year difference is measured birth date to birth date. This is distinct from intercourse under §261.5 and from §288(a)’s under-14 branch.

Under §288(c)(1), felony state prison for 1, 2, or 3 years, or misdemeanor county jail up to 364 days (§18.5). The felony lower term is one year, not the generic 16 months. Subdivision (a)’s bodily-harm life term and subdivision (e)’s additional fine are not automatically transferred to this branch. Registration under §290, other fines, and case-specific consequences require separate review.

## ca-sexual-penetration-289-a1a

Sexual penetration against the person’s will by force, violence, duress, menace, or fear of immediate unlawful bodily injury (§289(a)(1)(A)). The definition in §289(k) includes specified objects and body parts and a sexual purpose; the ordinary adult branch must be distinguished from the child-age branches.

Ordinary §289(a)(1)(A) term: 3, 6, or 8 years in state prison. The adjacent age-specific terms are 8, 10, or 12 years for a child under 14 (B), and 6, 8, or 10 years for a minor aged 14–17 (C). Verify the charged subdivision and age rather than applying the adult term to every case. This is not a total ceiling: §§269, 288.7, 667.61 and 667.6 can affect punishment when their conditions apply. Registration under §290, enhancements, fines, and individualized sentencing require separate review.
