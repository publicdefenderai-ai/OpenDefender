# California driving and vessels: publication batch

21 proposed choices from 11 primary sections. 17 research sections become monitored publication dependencies; 13 dependencies were already retained for publication.

- Publication covers only the 21 named branches; injury DUI, licensing, racing and other gaps remain open.
- No sentencing or enforceability uncertainty is silently treated as a minor-offense deferral.
- Pins and unchanged-source comparison are required for promoted research sources; acquisition time is never renewed by replay.
- Statutes govern punishment. Instruction matches are bounded and do not establish complete coverage.

## Review decisions

- VEH 2800.3(a) is a misdemeanor/felony alternative with 3/5/7-year felony terms; death under (b) is felony-only with 4/6/10-year terms. Neither uses the default felony range.
- VEH 20001 injury and death/permanent-serious-injury penalties are separate. The driver conduct is in (a); charging-paper aliases preserve that reference for both penalty branches. Nondriving owners/passengers remain open.
- PEN 193.5(b) expressly uses 16 months, 2 years or 4 years under 1170(h). The other vessel-manslaughter subdivisions have different custody rules.
- HNC 668(g) requires a fine with custody for operator injury under 655(f). HNC 668(e) expressly includes charter crew under 655.4, including its injury branch; operator felony punishment is not transferred to crew.
- PEN 193.8 concerns adult entrustment to an intoxicated minor, not the punishment for vessel manslaughter. PEN 499 concerns recidivist 499b punishment, not a standalone generic vessel-taking offense. Both remain research carry-forwards.
- HNC 655(e) addiction status and its treatment exception remain open. No generic intoxication choice silently includes that branch.

### Evading a peace officer in a motor vehicle: VEH 2800.1(a)

While driving, willfully fleeing or trying to evade a pursuing peace officer with intent to evade. The pursuing vehicle must display a lighted red lamp visible from the front that the driver sees or reasonably should see, use a siren as reasonably necessary, be distinctively marked, and be driven by an officer in a distinctive uniform.

Misdemeanor: up to 364 days in county jail. Penal Code section 672 permits an additional fine up to $1,000 where no other fine is prescribed. These are adult base penalties. Enhancements, restitution, assessments, probation and case-specific sentencing rules can change the outcome.

### Hit-and-run involving injury: VEH 20001(b)(1)

A driver involved in an accident causing injury to another person fails to stop and perform the identification, reasonable-assistance and applicable reporting duties in sections 20001(a), 20003 and 20004. The driver must know of the accident and know of injury or know facts making injury reasonably probable. Causing the collision or being at fault is not required. This entry is for the driver; a nondriving owner or controlling passenger requires separate analysis. Death or permanent serious injury uses the separate subdivision (b)(2) penalty.

Misdemeanor or felony: up to 364 days in county jail or 16 months, 2 years or 3 years in state prison under Penal Code section 18. A fine of $1,000 to $10,000 may be imposed alone or with imprisonment; the court may reduce the minimum fine based on ability to pay and stated interests-of-justice reasons. Section 20001(c)'s separate five-year flight enhancement requires a qualifying manslaughter conviction and a charged and admitted or proven allegation; it is not automatic for every hit-and-run. These are adult base penalties. Enhancements, restitution, assessments, probation and case-specific sentencing rules can change the outcome.

Mental state: Knowledge of the accident and actual or reasonably probable injury, followed by willful failure to perform a required duty.
Dependencies: PEN:17, PEN:18, PEN:18.5, PEN:191.5, PEN:192, VEH:20003, VEH:20004.
