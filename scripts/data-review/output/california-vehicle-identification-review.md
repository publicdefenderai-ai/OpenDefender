# California vehicle identification and remaining duties: publication batch

10 proposed choices from 9 primary sections. 3 research sections become monitored publication dependencies; 7 dependencies were already retained for publication.

- Publication covers only the ten named branches; owner/passenger duties and other gaps remain expressly open.
- No sentencing or enforceability uncertainty is silently treated as a minor-offense deferral.
- Pins and unchanged-source comparison are required for promoted research sources; acquisition time is never renewed by replay.
- Statutes govern punishment. Instruction matches are bounded and do not establish complete coverage.

## Review decisions

- Ten distinct choices preserve each actor, mental state, purpose and punishment. This does not close the vehicle chapter.
- 10803 requires more than one vehicle or parts from more than one vehicle. The 10804 scrap-processing and recovered-property exceptions are dependencies, not new crimes.
- 10802 can apply to one vehicle or part. Its concealment intent and sale/transfer/import/export purpose distinguish it from 10750.
- The 10803(a) 2/4/6-year and $60,000 felony alternatives are not transferred to possession under (b), whose alternatives are 16 months/2/3 years and $30,000.
- 10751(a) is a misdemeanor under 40000.9. Its separate civil property-disposition process is not presented as a criminal charge.
- 20002(b) concerns the person who parked a runaway vehicle. It does not resolve nondriving owner/passenger-in-control liability.
- The source discrepancy involving the missing HSC 113785 definition is recorded as CA-007. Absence does not prove repeal or authorize silent substitution.

## Proposed choices

### Knowingly owning or operating a chop shop: VEH 10801

Knowingly and intentionally owning or operating premises meeting Vehicle Code 250's chop-shop definition. The activity concerns vehicles or parts known to be illegally obtained by theft, fraud or conspiracy to defraud, for the specified identity-concealment or sale/disposal purpose. An ordinary repair or dismantling business is not a chop shop merely because it handles parts.

Misdemeanor or felony. Felony punishment: 2, 3 or 4 years under Penal Code 1170(h), a fine up to $50,000, or both. Misdemeanor punishment: up to 364 days in county jail, a fine up to $1,000, or both. Section 1170(h) custody is ordinarily in county jail, subject to its state-prison exclusions. The felony fine ceiling does not become the misdemeanor fine ceiling. Adult base penalties are shown; enhancements, assessments, restitution and case-specific sentencing rules require separate review.

Mental state: Knowing and intentional ownership or operation, with the illegal-origin knowledge and purpose required by the statutory chop-shop definition.
Dependencies: PEN:1170, PEN:17, PEN:18.5, VEH:250.

The felony fine ceiling does not become the misdemeanor fine ceiling. Adult base penalties are shown; enhancements, assessments, restitution and case-specific sentencing rules require separate review.
