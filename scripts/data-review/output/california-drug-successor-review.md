# California combined drug follow-up

18 proposed choices from 14 previously retained primary sections. Reuses 38 sources and adds one licensing-protection dependency. Configured California choices: 236; current eligibility and production deployment remain separate.

- All 16 prior instruction-gap entries now have bounded matches, not a statewide completeness score.
- Four partial juvenile-actor instruction matches, two absent-source references, HSC 11395 standalone identity and remaining chapter research remain.
- New cannabis summaries preserve ordinary fines and the literal additional-fine authority rather than promising an all-in $500 cap.
- Independent legal review is pending. The original source-refresh clock and changed-source holds remain.

## Proposed choices

### Adult unlawful cultivation of more than six cannabis plants: HSC §11358

For a person age 18 or older, unlawfully planting, cultivating, harvesting, drying or processing more than six living cannabis plants under subdivisions (c)-(d). Exactly six plants does not satisfy this greater-than-six branch. Under-18 cases and the age-18-to-20 six-or-fewer infraction are outside this entry. Lawful adult-use, medical and licensed commercial activity must be distinguished from unlawful conduct. A license alone does not excuse acts outside its state/local authorization and governing requirements.

Mental state: Knowing unlawful cultivation and the adult-age/plant-count conditions; a felony branch requires its additional statutory facts.

Ordinary subdivision (c): up to 6 months in county jail, a fine up to $500, or both. Subdivision (d) permits a felony term of 16 months, 2 years or 3 years under Penal Code section 1170(h) only for a qualifying serious/registrable prior, two or more prior subdivision (c) convictions, or the specified environmental conditions in (d)(3). Those conditions require the listed statutory violation or intentional/grossly negligent substantial environmental harm; an environmental concern alone is insufficient. Terms under Penal Code section 1170(h) are ordinarily served in county jail, subject to its state-prison exclusions. The $500 figure is the ordinary subdivision (c) fine, not a complete calculation for a felony case. A drug-program fee up to $150 under section 11372.7 depends on ability to pay. Adult base penalties are shown; enhancements, restitution, assessments and case-specific sentencing rules may change the result.

### Adult unlawful possession of cannabis for sale: HSC §11359

For a person age 18 or older, knowingly possessing a usable amount of cannabis with intent that it be unlawfully sold. Possession for personal use is not possession for sale. Under-18 cases use a separate route. Lawful adult-use, medical and licensed commercial activity must be distinguished from unlawful conduct. A license alone does not excuse acts outside its state/local authorization and governing requirements.

Mental state: Knowledge of the cannabis and intent to sell; each higher-penalty age, prior-conviction or knowing-minor-sale condition must be established.

Ordinary subdivision (b): up to 6 months in county jail, a fine up to $500, or both. A 16-month, 2-year or 3-year felony term under section 1170(h) is possible under (c) for a qualifying serious/registrable prior, two prior (b) convictions, or knowing sale/attempted sale to a minor. Subdivision (d) separately covers an actor age 21 or older knowingly using a person age 20 or younger in the specified unlawful cannabis activities. Terms under Penal Code section 1170(h) are ordinarily served in county jail, subject to its state-prison exclusions. Section 11372(a) also lists this offense for an additional fine up to $20,000; the $500 ordinary fine is not presented as an all-in monetary ceiling. A drug-program fee up to $150 under section 11372.7 depends on ability to pay. Adult base penalties are shown; enhancements, restitution, assessments and case-specific sentencing rules may change the result.

## Shared-source decisions

- Cannabis authorization: BPC 26032 requires state/local authorization and compliance; it is not blanket immunity for every licensee act.
- Cannabis fines: ordinary $500 provisions and HSC 11372(a)'s additional-fine text are disclosed together; $500 is not represented as an aggregate ceiling.
- False compartments: the retained CALCRIM 2441 text incorporates the factory-equipment limitation from [People v. Arias (2008)](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ca-supreme-court/1030162.html). An unchanged factory space is insufficient.
- Manufacturing offers: current retained HSC 11379.6(e) controls; CALCRIM 2331's (c) citation remains literal in the source artifact and indexed as CA-005.
- Attorney fees: section 11370.6(b) adds proof requirements; 11370.9(g) provides a distinct fee exclusion. They must not be collapsed into one rule.
- Precursors: manufacturing intent and knowing transfer to a manufacturer have different penalties. The listed chemicals and branch-specific intended drug control; parallel sections are not assumed identical.
