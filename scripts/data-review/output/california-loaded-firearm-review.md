# California loaded-firearm carrying branches

7 grading choices share one primary section; 22 reused dependencies and 22 newly monitored sections. No receipt extension.

- Seven choices share the conduct in section 25850(a) and distinguish the grading factors in (c)(1)-(7). They are not seven independent crimes to stack for one act. Overlapping allegations require case-specific treatment.
- The first four branches declare felonies without an express section 1170(h) alternative; the next two expressly provide it. The no-factor misdemeanor branch does not override a proven factor.

## Penal Code 25850(c)(1): Carrying a loaded firearm: prior felony or specified weapons conviction

Carrying a loaded firearm on the person or in a vehicle in a public place or public street in an incorporated city, or in a prohibited area of unincorporated territory. The prosecution must prove knowledge that the person was carrying a firearm; knowledge that it was loaded is not required. The applicable loaded-firearm definition requires ammunition in a firing position, including a loaded magazine or clip attached to the firearm; merely having ammunition nearby is not enough. A muzzle-loader has a separate capped-or-primed, powder-and-projectile rule. The additional factor is a prior felony conviction or a conviction under a provision listed in section 16580; a generic assertion of any criminal record is insufficient. Applicable authorizations and exceptions must be checked, including qualifying official duties, licensed carrying, specified employment or sporting activities, and the conditions governing a residence, campsite, business, property or immediate danger. These are conditional exceptions, not blanket permission to carry in public. Constitutional litigation and applicable court orders require separate case-specific review.

Felony: ordinarily 16 months, 2 years or 3 years in state prison under Penal Code section 18(a). This branch declares a felony without the express section 1170(h) alternative used in paragraphs (5) and (6); do not automatically substitute that county-jail sentencing route. If no other offense-specific fine is prescribed, section 672 permits an additional discretionary base fine up to $10,000. Section 25850(d) generally requires at least three months in county jail for specified prior offenses under sections 23515 or 16580, including as a probation condition; the statute allows an unusual-case exception with reasons recorded by the court. Other firearm offenses, restrictions, assessments and case-specific sentencing rules require separate review.
