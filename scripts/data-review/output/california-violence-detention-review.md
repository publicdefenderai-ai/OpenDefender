# California serious violence, abuse and detention: combined publication batch

28 proposed choices from 18 primary sections; 26 reused dependencies, 44 newly monitored sections including 36 promoted from research. 25 additional instruction entries receive bounded matches.

- Twenty-eight named branches only; same-section siblings remain open.
- Published felony false-imprisonment custody does not resolve its expressly uncertain fine limit.
- Adult base penalties and specified conditional increases are not a complete sentence calculation or a promise of parole.
- Research promotion requires unchanged-source comparison. Replay does not renew the acquisition time or receipt expiry.

## Review decisions

- 273ab is a distinct section from 273a. Citation parsing, URLs and runtime dependency keys retain all trailing letters.
- 203 uses the penalty in 204; 206 uses 206.1, including its January 2026 child/caregiver parole-minimum change. No term is inferred from a nearby offense.
- 220 separates ordinary intent, the under-18 sexual-intent branch and first-degree burglary. Mayhem is not silently included in the latter two lists.
- 273a indirect-harm branches require criminal negligence; actual great bodily injury is not required merely because the circumstances create the specified risk.
- 273.5 prior-dependent terms require the exact prior offense and seven-year window. A generic prior-conviction answer cannot choose the higher range.
- 368 preserves victim-age and injury/death distinctions and its restrictions on stacking enhancements. Financial branches remain research.
- 207(a)/(b), 209(a)/(b), and 209.5 have different movement, intent, victim and punishment requirements. The ordinary under-14 increase has parent/court-access exceptions.
- Ordinary false imprisonment and the aggravated felony branch are separate choices. The latter is not described as a discretionary misdemeanor simply because the neighboring sentence has a jail alternative.
- 236.1 distinguishes forced labor, specified predicate exploitation and minor commercial-sex conduct. The 236.4 additional fine and injury/prior terms are disclosed rather than hidden behind the base penalty.
- 278 and 278.5 distinguish custody from visitation and retain the conditional 278.7 immediate-harm protection; the exception is not an instruction to disregard an order.

### Mayhem: PEN 203

Unlawfully and maliciously causing a bodily loss, disabling injury, disfigurement, or injury to the tongue, eye, nose, ear or lip specified in Penal Code 203. The type and duration of the injury matter; not every serious injury is mayhem.

Felony: 2, 4 or 8 years in state prison under section 204. A fine up to $10,000 may also be imposed under section 672. Adult base penalties are shown. Enhancements, restitution, assessments, probation eligibility and case-specific sentencing rules require separate review.

Mental state: Malicious conduct causing the specified injury; aggravated mayhem has a distinct specific-intent requirement.

Other sentencing rules can require longer; parole is not guaranteed. Adult base penalties are shown. Enhancements, restitution, assessments, probation eligibility and case-specific sentencing rules require separate review.

Mental state: Specific intent to cause cruel or extreme pain for one of the statutory purposes.

### Corporal injury to a spouse, partner or co-parent: PEN 273.5(a)

Willfully inflicting physical injury resulting in a traumatic condition on a current/former spouse, cohabitant, fiance or dating partner, or the other parent of the person's child. Probation, prior-related jail minimums and protective orders require separate review; any prior conviction does not automatically trigger these increases. Adult base penalties are shown. Enhancements, restitution, assessments, probation eligibility and case-specific sentencing rules require separate review.

Mental state: Willful direct infliction of injury causing a traumatic condition.

### Elder or dependent-adult abuse likely to cause great bodily harm or death: PEN 368(b)(1)

An elder is age 65 or older; a dependent adult is an adult age 18-64 meeting the statutory disability, limitation or inpatient-facility definition. Willful unjustifiable suffering, injury or endangerment under circumstances likely to produce great bodily harm or death, with knowledge or reason to know the person's protected status. Custody and criminal-negligence requirements depend on the charged direct or indirect-harm branch.

Misdemeanor or felony: up to 364 days in county jail, a fine up to $6,000, or both under the county-jail/fine alternative; the felony prison terms are 2, 3 or 4 years. Section 368(j) restricts overlapping injury/death enhancements. Do not add all these terms together automatically. Adult base penalties are shown. Enhancements, restitution, assessments, probation eligibility and case-specific sentencing rules require separate review.

Mental state: Willful conduct; knowledge or reason to know elder/dependent status; criminal negligence where required by an indirect-harm branch.
Dependencies: HSC:1250, HSC:1250.2, HSC:1250.3, PEN:12022.7, PEN:17, PEN:18.5.

### Child abduction by depriving custody or visitation: PEN 278.5(a)

Taking, enticing, keeping, withholding or concealing a child under 18 while maliciously depriving a lawful custodian of custody or another person of a lawful visitation right. Section 278.7 provides a conditional immediate-harm protection with reporting and custody-proceeding requirements; it is not blanket permission to disregard a custody order.
