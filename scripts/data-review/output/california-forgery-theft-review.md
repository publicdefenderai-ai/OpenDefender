### Forgery of record entries or record instruments: §471

Making, forging, or altering a record-book entry or an instrument purporting to be a record or return specified in section 470, with intent to defraud another.

Mental state: Intent to defraud.

Felony alternative: 16 months, 2 years, or 3 years under section 1170(h); misdemeanor alternative: up to 364 days in county jail. Section 17 governs misdemeanor treatment. Section 473(b) requires misdemeanor treatment for forgery involving a listed check, bond, bank bill, note, cashier’s check, traveler’s check, or money order valued at $950 or less, subject to its specified prior-conviction exceptions. That reduction does not apply when the person is convicted of both forgery and identity theft under section 530.5. Instrument type and value must be checked; the rule is not a blanket reduction of every forgery. These are adult base penalties, not a total sentence prediction. Restitution, assessments, enhancements and case-specific sentencing rules require separate review.

Dependencies: PEN:1170, PEN:17, PEN:18.5, PEN:290, PEN:470, PEN:473, PEN:530.5, PEN:667, PEN:672.

Although the text calls the offense a felony, its alternate county-jail punishment permits misdemeanor treatment under section 17. These are adult base penalties, not a total sentence prediction. Restitution, assessments, enhancements and case-specific sentencing rules require separate review.

Dependencies: PEN:1170, PEN:17, PEN:18.5, PEN:672.
