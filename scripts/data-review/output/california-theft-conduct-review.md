## Published explanation

Theft meeting the petty-theft rules in §§484 and 490.2, ordinarily involving property worth $950 or less. Firearm theft is excluded from §490.2. An infraction is a narrow option under §490.1 for property worth $50 or less with no theft-related prior conviction; it is not the default. Check any prior-conviction allegation and the exact charging statute, including §666.1. The alleged form of theft matters. Larceny involves taking another person's property without consent, intending at the time to deprive the owner permanently or for long enough to lose a major part of its value or enjoyment, and moving and keeping it even briefly. Theft by trick involves knowing the property belongs to someone else, obtaining possession through fraud or deceit with that deprivation intent, and keeping it for some time; the owner did not intend to transfer ownership. Theft by false pretense instead involves knowing and intentional deception to induce transfer of both possession and ownership, with the owner's actual reliance on the deception. False pretense also has special proof requirements: a false writing or token, a signed or handwritten memorandum, or two witnesses or one witness with corroborating evidence, as specified in section 532(b). A broken promise alone does not prove an original intent to deceive. These are alternative theories, not a combined checklist that must all be proved; the charging papers and facts determine which theory is alleged. Other theft theories, defenses and special proof rules may require separate review.
