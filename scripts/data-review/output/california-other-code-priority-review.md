## BPC 2052(a): Practicing or holding out as practicing medicine without lawful authority

Practicing or attempting to practice a system of treating the sick, advertising or holding oneself out as doing so, or diagnosing, treating, operating or prescribing for a physical or mental condition without the required valid authorization. A missing California physician certificate alone does not decide every case: other professional authorization, qualifying complementary services with required disclosures, emergencies and specified consultation or training exceptions can matter. The exact activity and conditions must be checked.

Misdemeanor alternative: up to 364 days in county jail. Felony alternative: ordinarily 16 months, 2 years or 3 years under Penal Code section 1170(h), subject to its state-prison exclusions. Section 2052 permits a base fine up to $10,000, alone or with either custody alternative. Section 2052 expressly applies notwithstanding section 146; this entry does not substitute that section’s general infraction treatment. Assessments, restitution and separate charges remain additional considerations.

## BPC 7028(a)(1): Acting as a contractor without a required license

Engaging in business or acting as a contractor without the license required by the Contractors State License Law. The definition includes offering, bidding or undertaking covered construction work, as well as performing it. Licensing exemptions must be checked. The small-project exemption requires an aggregate price below $1,000, no required building permit, no employees assisting the work, and no disqualifying contractor advertising; splitting a larger project does not qualify. Separate owner-builder, employee and other statutory exemptions have their own conditions.

First conviction: up to six months in county jail, a base fine up to $5,000, or both. For a repeat conviction under section 7028(c), the fine is the greatest of $5,000, 20% of the contract price or 20% of aggregate payments, with at least 90 days in jail unless the court states an unusual-case reason for less; this summary does not supply an unstated upper custody limit for that branch. A third or subsequent conviction under (d), or a qualifying prior license revocation under (e), carries 90 to 364 days and a fine from $5,000 up to the greatest of $10,000 or the two 20% measures. Restitution for economic losses can apply even if the customer knew the contractor was unlicensed. Assessments and other charges are separate.

[Official primary section](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=7028.)

## LAB 3700.5(a): Failing to secure required workers compensation

Failing to secure payment of workers compensation while knowing, or reasonably being expected to know from one’s knowledge or experience, of that obligation. Authorized insurance and qualifying consent to self-insure are statutory alternatives; absence of a commercial policy alone does not prove the offense. Employer status and the applicable duty must be established.

Misdemeanor: up to 364 days in county jail, a fine measured at up to twice the unpaid premium but not less than $10,000, or both. For a second or subsequent conviction, section 3700.5(b) provides up to 364 days, a fine of triple the unpaid premium but not less than $50,000, or both. The court determines the premium measure; these provisions do not supply a flat $10,000 or $50,000 maximum. Investigation costs may be charged on a first conviction and must be charged on a subsequent conviction; benefits, reimbursement and assessments remain separate.

[Official primary section](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=3700.5.)

## LAB 6425(a): Willful workplace safety violation causing death or prolonged impairment

An employer or an employee with direction, management, control or custody of employment, the workplace or another employee willfully violates an applicable occupational safety or health standard, order or special order, or Health and Safety Code section 25910, and that violation causes an employee’s death or permanent or prolonged bodily impairment. An accident or a workplace injury alone does not establish this offense. Section 6425(e) uses Penal Code section 7’s definition of willfully; the precise underlying rule and causation must be proved.

For an individual, the misdemeanor alternative is up to 364 days in county jail, a base fine up to $100,000, or both; the felony alternative is 16 months, 2 years or 3 years in state prison, a fine up to $250,000, or both. A corporation or limited liability company has a separate maximum fine of $1.5 million under (a). Qualifying prior convictions trigger the different branches in (b) or (c); they are not included in this ordinary-branch range. Other charges and assessments remain separate.

[Official primary section](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=6425.)

## LAB 6425(b): Workplace safety harm offense after specified prior safety conviction

Felony custody alternative: 16 months, 2 years or 3 years in state prison, a base fine up to $250,000, or both for an individual. The express fine-only alternative can permit misdemeanor treatment under Penal Code section 17; it does not create a new ordinary county-jail term in this subdivision.

## INS 1871.4(a)(1): Making a false material statement to obtain or deny workers compensation

Making or causing a knowingly false or fraudulent material statement or representation to obtain or deny workers compensation. Compensation includes benefits and payments for an injured worker and qualifying dependents, not just wage replacement.

Misdemeanor alternative: 364 days in county jail under Penal Code section 18.5. Felony alternative: 2, 3 or 5 years under section 1170(h), subject to its state-prison exclusions. The base fine may reach $150,000 or twice the value of the fraud, whichever is greater, and may accompany imprisonment. Restitution is mandatory; investigation costs may be imposed. Section 1871.4(c) provides a two-year enhancement for each qualifying prior felony conviction when alleged and admitted or proved; the qualifying prior and procedure require separate assessment. Assessments and other applicable charges remain separate.

## WIC 10980(a): False statements or attempts to obtain public assistance

Willfully and knowingly making a false statement with intent to deceive, or knowingly concealing a material fact, to obtain aid under the covered public social services provisions; also knowingly attempting to obtain or continue aid, or a larger amount, without entitlement. This branch is distinct from actually obtaining or retaining aid under subdivision (c). Section 10980(j) can bar prosecution for specified CalWORKs or CalFresh overpayments when the county had qualifying eligibility-match information and failed to provide timely, adequate notice; its 45-day rule includes authorized-delay exceptions. An overpayment alone is not proof of fraud.

Misdemeanor: up to six months in county jail, a base fine up to $500, or both. Restitution, benefit consequences, assessments and separate charges require individual assessment.

## UIC 2101(a): False statements or identification affecting unemployment or other covered benefits

Willfully making a false statement, knowingly failing to disclose a material fact, or using false identification to obtain, increase, reduce or defeat a benefit or payment for oneself or another under the state division or the federal unemployment, training, trade-readjustment or other allowance laws administered by the department. A simple payment error alone does not establish the specified conduct. The applicable program and material facts matter.

Under Unemployment Insurance Code section 2122, the misdemeanor alternative is up to 364 days in county jail; the felony alternative is ordinarily 16 months, 2 years or 3 years in state prison under Penal Code section 18. A base fine up to $20,000 may be imposed alone or with imprisonment. Section 2117’s separate misdemeanor-only penalty is not substituted for this offense. Restitution, administrative benefit consequences and other charges remain separate.
