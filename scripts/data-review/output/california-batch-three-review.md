## ca-rape-261-a1

Intercourse with a person unable to give legal consent because of a mental disorder or developmental or physical disability, where that incapacity was known or reasonably should have been known (§261(a)(1)). Disability alone does not establish incapacity; the prosecution must prove it, considering applicable mitigating measures and voluntary supports.

Ordinary §264(a) punishment: 3, 6, or 8 years in state prison. Section 264(b) also permits an ability-to-pay-based fine up to $70. This is not a total sentencing ceiling: specified allegations can trigger life terms, consecutive terms, or other provisions (§§667.61, 667.6, 269, 288.7). Registration under §290, other fines, enhancements, and the actual allegations require separate review.

## ca-murder-in-the-first-degree

Unlawful killing with the elements of murder under §§187–188 and a first-degree basis under §189(a), such as a willful, deliberate, premeditated killing. First degree is not limited to premeditation. Felony-murder participation is constrained by §189(e), subject to its §189(f) exception; participation alone does not automatically establish murder.

Ordinary term: 25 years to life in state prison (§190(a)). Statutorily established special circumstances can expose a person to life without parole or the death penalty (§190.2); those are not automatic consequences of every first-degree murder charge. Section 190.5 bars death for an offense committed under age 18. This describes statutory exposure, not current execution policy or a parole-release prediction. Age, allegations, enhancements, and applicable constitutional limits require individual legal review.

## ca-murder-in-the-second-degree

Murder under §§187–188 that is not first degree under §189. Express or implied malice can apply; an intent to kill is not required for every implied-malice theory. Malice cannot be imputed solely from participation in another crime, subject to the statutory felony-murder rule.

Ordinary term: 15 years to life (§190(a)). Section 190(b)–(c) provides 25 years to life or life without parole for specified peace-officer killings with the required duty, knowledge, and additional findings. Section 190(d) provides 20 years to life for its specified shooting from a vehicle with intent to inflict great bodily injury. These are conditional alternatives, not a predicted sentence; enhancements and parole rules require separate review.

## ca-attempted-murder

An intent to unlawfully kill and a direct but ineffectual act toward the killing (§§21a, 664). Attempted murder requires intent to kill; the implied-malice basis that can support a completed murder is not enough. The premeditation allegation and any protected-victim allegation must be distinguished.

Under §664(a), ordinarily 5, 7, or 9 years in state prison; willful, deliberate, premeditated attempted murder carries life with the possibility of parole when charged and admitted or found true. Section 664(e) instead imposes life with parole for its specified duty-related protected-victim cases with the required knowledge; §664(f) imposes 15 years to life when its additional premeditation requirements are met. Enhancements and actual parole eligibility require separate review.

## ca-involuntary-manslaughter

Unlawful killing without malice during a nonfelony unlawful act, or a potentially deadly lawful act done unlawfully or without due caution (§192(b)). This subdivision expressly excludes acts committed in driving a vehicle; those require the applicable vehicular provision.

Custody is ordinarily in county jail under that scheme, but statutory exclusions can require state prison; do not promise a custody location or split sentence without reviewing eligibility.
