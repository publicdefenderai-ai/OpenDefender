## 647(a): Engaging in lewd conduct in public

Willfully touching one's own or another person's genitals, buttocks or female breast for sexual arousal or gratification, or to annoy or offend, in a public place, a place open to the public or a place exposed to public view. Another person who might be offended must be present, and the defendant must know or reasonably should know of that person's presence. Actual offense to the observer need not be proved. Nudity alone is not the touching conduct described by this entry. This entry concerns engaging in the conduct; solicitation is a separate theory under the same subdivision.

Ordinarily up to six months in county jail, a base fine up to $1,000, or both under Penal Code section 19. Assessments, restitution, probation conditions and separate charges can affect the outcome.

## 647(a): Soliciting lewd conduct in public

Requesting another person to engage in touching of genitals, buttocks or female breast, intending sexual arousal or gratification or to annoy or offend. Both the request and the intended conduct must have the required public-place or public-view connection. The defendant must know or reasonably should know that someone likely to be present could be offended by the requested conduct. The request must be communicated to the solicited person; a disputed failure to receive it matters. This is not a general prohibition on sexual speech or a requirement that the requested touching actually occur.

Ordinarily up to six months in county jail, a base fine up to $1,000, or both under Penal Code section 19. Assessments, restitution, probation conditions and separate charges can affect the outcome.

[Official primary section](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=647.)

## 647(h): Loitering or prowling on private property with criminal purpose

Delaying, lingering, prowling or wandering on another person's private property without a lawful purpose for being there, with the purpose and intent to commit a crime if an opportunity arises. Mere presence, appearance or lack of visible business does not by itself establish that criminal purpose. This private-property offense is distinct from public intoxication, school loitering and peeking into an inhabited building.

Ordinarily up to six months in county jail, a base fine up to $1,000, or both under Penal Code section 19. Assessments, restitution, probation conditions and separate charges can affect the outcome.

[Official primary section](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=647.)

## 647(i): Peeking into an inhabited building while prowling on private property

While delaying, lingering, prowling or wandering on another person's private property without a lawful purpose for being there, peeking through the door or window of an inhabited building or structure. Inhabited means used as a dwelling; someone need not be inside at that moment. Unlike section 647(h), this branch does not require a separate intent to commit another crime if an opportunity arises. It is not the same as the device-recording or image-distribution branches in section 647(j).

Ordinarily up to six months in county jail, a base fine up to $1,000, or both under Penal Code section 19. Assessments, restitution, probation conditions and separate charges can affect the outcome.

[Official primary section](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=647.)

## 21310: Carrying a concealed dirk or dagger

Knowingly carrying a substantially concealed dirk or dagger on the person, while knowing the instrument can readily be used as a stabbing weapon capable of causing great bodily injury or death. Intent to use it as a weapon is not required. A qualifying pocketknife or folding knife is not a dirk or dagger unless its blade is exposed and locked into position. A knife in a sheath worn openly suspended from the waist is not concealed under section 20200. Qualified exceptions exist, including specified institutional, entertainment, law-enforcement and found-item circumstances; their exact conditions matter.

Misdemeanor alternative: up to 364 days in county jail under section 18.5. Felony alternative: ordinarily 16 months, 2 years or 3 years under section 1170(h), subject to its state-prison exclusions. Where no other fine is prescribed, section 672 permits an additional discretionary base fine up to $1,000 for a misdemeanor or $10,000 for a felony. Assessments, restitution, enhancements and case-specific sentencing rules remain separate.

[Official primary section](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=21310.)

## 21510(a): Possessing a switchblade in the driver or passenger area of a vehicle

The knife must have a blade at least two inches long and meet section 17235's automatic-opening definition. The statutory exception for qualifying one-hand thumb-pressure knives with a detent or closing bias matters; not every folding or one-hand-opening knife is a switchblade. Knowledge of the knife's presence and switchblade characteristics is required, but intent to use it as a weapon is not. This branch concerns possession in the driver's or passenger's area of a motor vehicle in a public place or place open to the public, including interior compartments within that area. Possession can involve control or the right to control, without physically holding the knife. It does not make all possession in every location the same offense.

Ordinarily up to six months in county jail, a base fine up to $1,000, or both under Penal Code section 19. Assessments, restitution, probation conditions and separate charges can affect the outcome.

[Official primary section](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=21510.)

## 21510(c): Selling, offering, lending or transferring a switchblade

The knife must have a blade at least two inches long and meet section 17235's automatic-opening definition. The statutory exception for qualifying one-hand thumb-pressure knives with a detent or closing bias matters; not every folding or one-hand-opening knife is a switchblade. Knowledge of the knife's presence and switchblade characteristics is required, but intent to use it as a weapon is not. This branch covers selling, offering or exposing for sale, lending, transferring or giving the knife to another person. Offering or exposing for sale additionally requires intent to sell. The statutory identity is separate from the vehicle-possession and carrying branches; an item's appearance alone does not establish its mechanical characteristics.

Ordinarily up to six months in county jail, a base fine up to $1,000, or both under Penal Code section 19. Assessments, restitution, probation conditions and separate charges can affect the outcome.
