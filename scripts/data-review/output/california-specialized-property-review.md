### Fraudulent possession of an access card: §484e(c)

Acquiring or keeping an access card without the cardholder’s or issuer’s consent, intending to defraud and to use, sell, or transfer it to someone other than the cardholder or issuer. An access card includes qualifying account numbers and other means of account access defined in section 484d. This entry covers subdivision (c), not the separately graded transfer, multiple-card, or account-information branches.

Mental state: Intent to defraud and intent to use, sell, or transfer the card as specified; possession alone is insufficient.

Ordinary petty theft: up to 6 months in county jail, a fine up to $1,000, or both under section 490. Section 490.1 permits an infraction with a fine up to $250 when the value does not exceed $50 and the person has no other theft or theft-related conviction. Qualifying prior convictions can permit felony treatment under section 666.1: up to 364 days in county jail or 16 months, 2 years, or 3 years under section 1170(h); a second or subsequent section 666.1 conviction carries a county-jail or state-prison alternative. The charging document and prior history matter. These are adult base penalties; enhancements, restitution, assessments, and case-specific sentencing rules may change the result.

Dependencies: PEN:1170, PEN:17, PEN:18, PEN:18.5, PEN:484d, PEN:490, PEN:490.1, PEN:666.1.
