## ca-assault-240

An unlawful attempt, with present ability, to apply force to another person. An actual touching is not required; the alleged conduct and mental state still matter.

Ordinary assault under §240: up to 6 months in county jail, a base fine up to $1,000, or both under §241(a). Protected-victim variants require separate review.

## ca-domestic-battery

Battery against a person in a relationship listed in §243(e)(1), including a spouse, cohabitant, co-parent, former spouse, fiancé, or current or former dating or engagement partner. Check the actual relationship and alleged touching.

Under §243(e)(1), up to 364 days in county jail (§18.5), a base fine up to $2,000, or both. Probation or a suspended sentence requires at least a year of batterer treatment or approved alternative counseling; qualifying prior convictions can trigger a 48-hour minimum, subject to the statutory exception. Other probation payments and conditions may apply.

## ca-assault-with-deadly-weapon

Assault with a deadly weapon or instrument other than a firearm under §245(a)(1). Force likely to cause great bodily injury without a deadly weapon is a different branch, §245(a)(4); do not substitute this record for that charge.

For non-firearm deadly-weapon assault under §245(a)(1): 2, 3, or 4 years in state prison, or county jail up to 364 days (§18.5), or a base fine up to $10,000, or both fine and imprisonment as authorized. This is not a §1170(h) felony term. Section 245(e) requires confiscation of a covered weapon owned by the convicted person. Force-likely assault without a deadly weapon is a separate branch, §245(a)(4).

## ca-menacing

A willful threat of death or great bodily injury intended to be taken as a threat, with the specificity and immediacy required by §422(a), causing reasonable sustained fear for personal or immediate-family safety. Intent to carry out the threat is not required.

Criminal threats under §422(a): county jail up to 364 days (§18.5), or a state-prison term of 16 months, 2 years, or 3 years under §18(a). The state-prison alternative is not a §1170(h) term. These are base custody limits; fines and other sentencing consequences require separate review.

## ca-resisting-arrest

Willfully resisting, delaying, or obstructing a covered officer or emergency medical technician in the lawful performance of duties under §148(a)(1). Lawful recording alone is not a violation. Weapon-taking allegations require a different subdivision.

For §148(a)(1), when no other punishment is prescribed: county jail up to 364 days (§18.5), a base fine up to $1,000, or both. Weapon-taking and other subdivisions require separate review. Lawful recording of an officer does not by itself violate this provision (§148(g)).

## ca-protective-order-273-6-a

An intentional and knowing violation of an order covered by §273.6(a). Check the actual order, notice, and prohibited conduct. Injury and repeat-violation allegations can invoke other subdivisions.

Ordinary violation under §273.6(a): county jail up to 364 days (§18.5), a base fine up to $1,000, or both. Injury and qualifying repeat violations have different minimums or felony exposure under other subdivisions. Probation must be consistent with §1203.097; shelter payments and victim reimbursement may also apply.

## ca-false-info-to-police

Falsely identifying oneself as another or a fictitious person during lawful detention or arrest, for the evasion purpose specified in §148.9. This is not a general rule requiring everyone to answer police questions. Officer requirements differ between subdivisions.

False identification under §148.9 is a misdemeanor with the ordinary §19 limits: county jail up to 6 months, a base fine up to $1,000, or both. The statute requires a lawful detention or arrest and the specified evasion purpose; officer-status requirements differ between subdivisions (a) and (b).
