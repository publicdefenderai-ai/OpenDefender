## ca-computer-crime-502-c1

Knowingly accessing and, without permission, altering, damaging, deleting, destroying, or otherwise using computer data or systems to defraud, deceive, extort, or wrongfully control or obtain money, property, or data (§502(c)(1)). Unauthorized access alone is not this specific branch. The employment exceptions in §502(h)–(i) require separate attention.

Both §502(c)(1) and (c)(5) use §502(d)(1): misdemeanor jail up to 364 days (§18.5), a base fine up to $5,000, or both; or felony imprisonment under §1170(h) for 16 months, 2 years, or 3 years and a base fine up to $10,000. This branch has no $950 loss threshold for the felony alternative. Civil damages and forfeiture under §502.01 are separate; custody location, restitution, and other consequences require individual review.

## ca-identity-theft-530-5-c1

Acquiring or retaining another person’s identifying information with intent to defraud (§530.5(c)(1), definitions in §530.55). Actual use is not the act specified by this possession branch. A prior §530.5 conviction or information of 10 or more people belongs to the separate (c)(2)/(c)(3) branches.

This (c)(1) branch is a misdemeanor: a fine, up to 364 days in county jail (§18.5), or both. Section 530.5(c)(1) does not specify a fine amount. Do not import the felony alternative from (c)(2) or (c)(3) without that separate charged basis. Restitution and other consequences require individual review.

## ca-insurance-fraud-550-a1

Knowingly presenting or causing presentation of a false or fraudulent claim for payment of loss or injury (§550(a)(1)), including the forms of participation specified in (a). This false-claim branch differs from a false material statement under (b)(1).

Felony under §550(c)(1): 2, 3, or 5 years under §1170(h), and a base fine up to $50,000 or twice the fraud amount, whichever is greater. The $950 health-care-claim rule in (c)(2) does not supply a misdemeanor alternative for (a)(1). Restitution is mandatory under §550(c)(4). Qualifying pleaded/proved priors can add two-year enhancements under (e) and restrict probation under (d); (i) doubles fines for qualifying auto-insurance claims in a designated fraud crisis area. These are base terms, not total exposure.

## ca-insurance-fraud-550-b1

Presenting or causing a written or oral statement in support of or opposition to an insurance claim, knowing it contains false or misleading information about a material fact (§550(b)(1)); the participation language in (b) also applies. Verify this branch rather than selecting the separate false-claim branch.

Misdemeanor or felony under §550(c)(3): the misdemeanor alternative permits up to 364 days in county jail (§18.5), a base fine up to $10,000, or both. The felony alternative permits 2, 3, or 5 years under §1170(h), a base fine up to $50,000 or twice the fraud amount, whichever is greater, or both. Restitution is mandatory under §550(c)(4). Qualifying pleaded/proved priors can add two-year enhancements under (e) and restrict probation under (d); (i) doubles fines for qualifying auto-insurance claims in a designated fraud crisis area. These are base terms, not total exposure.

## ca-money-laundering

Conducting or attempting financial-institution transactions totaling over $5,000 within seven days or over $25,000 within 30 days, with the criminal-promotion intent or proceeds knowledge required by §186.10(a). Definitions and exclusions in §186.9 matter; merely handling money is not enough. The statute adds a special proof requirement for attorneys accepting criminal-defense fees.

Misdemeanor jail up to 364 days (§18.5), or felony imprisonment under §1170(h), ordinarily 16 months, 2 years, or 3 years. Section 186.10(a) permits a fine up to the greater of $250,000 or twice the value transacted, or imprisonment and fine; for a second or later conviction the fine ceiling becomes the greater of $500,000 or five times the value. Subdivision (c) adds 1–4 years for specified transaction-value bands when the required facts are charged and admitted or found true. Exact boundaries, aggregation, custody location, and other consequences require individual review; the base term is not a total ceiling.

## ca-forgery

Forgery under §470 covers specified acts such as signing another or a fictitious name without authority, counterfeiting handwriting or seals, falsifying records, or making or passing specified false instruments, with the required intent to defraud and knowledge. The charged act and instrument determine which branch applies.

Punishment comes from §473: ordinarily misdemeanor jail up to 364 days (§18.5) or felony imprisonment under §1170(h), ordinarily 16 months, 2 years, or 3 years. For the seven listed monetary instruments valued at $950 or less, §473(b) requires misdemeanor treatment unless its specified serious-prior/registration exceptions apply. That subsection also excludes a person convicted of both forgery and §530.5 identity theft; applying that exception to particular convictions requires legal review. Do not apply the $950 rule to every kind of forged document. Fines, restitution, custody location, and other consequences require separate review.

## ca-check-fraud

Willfully making, drawing, uttering, or delivering a check, draft, or order with intent to defraud, knowing sufficient funds or credit are lacking to cover it and other outstanding instruments (§476a(a)). An unpaid or bounced check alone does not establish intent and knowledge.

Under §476a(a), misdemeanor jail up to 364 days (§18.5) or felony imprisonment under §1170(h), ordinarily 16 months, 2 years, or 3 years. Under (b), a charged-and-convicted aggregate of $950 or less ordinarily receives misdemeanor treatment. The text contains specified serious-prior/registration exceptions, a three-or-more listed California conviction exception, and separately worded out-of-state/federal conviction exceptions. Do not reduce these to a universal three-prior rule; exact prior matching requires legal review. Fines, restitution, custody location, and other consequences remain separate.

## ca-credit-card-fraud

Access-card theft under §484g requires intent to defraud and the specified unauthorized or fraudulent card/account use or representation. More than $950 obtained in any consecutive six-month period constitutes grand theft under this section. Cross-references to §§484e–484f describe certain unlawfully obtained or altered cards; those sections are not interchangeable charges.

Ordinary petty theft under §490 carries up to 6 months in county jail, a base fine up to $1,000, or both. Ordinary non-firearm grand theft under §489(c) permits up to 364 days (§18.5) or §1170(h) imprisonment, ordinarily 16 months, 2 years, or 3 years. Section 490.1 permits an infraction (base fine up to $250, no jail) for qualifying theft of $50 or less with no theft-related prior, at prosecutorial discretion. Sections 490.2 and 666.1 contain prior-conviction exceptions that can change the ordinary low-value misdemeanor result. Verify charged theory, value, aggregation, priors, and custody eligibility; restitution and other consequences remain separate.

## ca-embezzlement

Fraudulently appropriating property entrusted to the accused (§503). Section 514 supplies punishment according to the value or kind of property, with a separate public-funds rule. A breach of trust or failure to repay alone does not establish every element of fraudulent appropriation.

Ordinary petty theft under §490 carries up to 6 months in county jail, a base fine up to $1,000, or both. Ordinary non-firearm grand theft under §489(c) permits up to 364 days (§18.5) or §1170(h) imprisonment, ordinarily 16 months, 2 years, or 3 years. Section 490.1 permits an infraction (base fine up to $250, no jail) for qualifying theft of $50 or less with no theft-related prior, at prosecutorial discretion. Sections 490.2 and 666.1 contain prior-conviction exceptions that can change the ordinary low-value misdemeanor result. Verify charged theory, value, aggregation, priors, and custody eligibility; restitution and other consequences remain separate. Section 514 separately classifies embezzlement of specified public funds as a state-prison felony and disqualifies the convicted person from public office; that branch needs specific review rather than assuming an ordinary petty-theft result. Firearm property also requires the distinct §489(a)/§490.2(c) treatment.
