# California DUI, licensing and racing: publication batch

24 proposed choices from 12 primary sections. 16 research sections become monitored publication dependencies; 30 dependencies were already retained for publication.

- Publication covers only the 24 named branches; addiction-status DUI, under-21 alcohol, owner/passenger duties and other gaps remain open.
- No sentencing or enforceability uncertainty is silently treated as a minor-offense deferral.
- Pins and unchanged-source comparison are required for promoted research sources; acquisition time is never renewed by replay.
- Statutes govern punishment. Instruction matches are bounded and do not establish complete coverage.

## Review decisions

- VEH 23153 requires a concurrent unlawful act or neglected duty that causes injury to someone other than the driver; a DUI label alone does not establish injury DUI.
- Mandatory base fines accompany custody under the DUI and specified repeat-license provisions. Probation has separate statutory minimums and program alternatives; ordinary minimums are not represented as unavoidable in every case.
- PEN 17(d)/19.8 permit infraction treatment of VEH 14601.1, 23109(c), and 40508. Infractions have no custody; the filing and disposition control. Other neighboring provisions do not inherit the alternative.
- Both operative and future versions of 13352, 23103.5, 23573 and 23575 are retained. The 2026 operative versions of 23573 and 23575 repeal January 1, 2033; the separately retained future versions are not substituted based on identical effective dates or active flags. Known transitions withhold dependent records until re-reviewed, even if an archive receipt is renewed.
- VEH 23109(i)(2) states January 1, 2029 for its sideshow-specific license provision. That future provision is not represented as operative in 2026.
- CA-006 preserves VEH 14601.1(c)'s literal 12500(d) reference; the retained offstreet-parking definition appears in 12500(c). No source text is rewritten.
- CALCRIM 2220 explains notice as a permissive inference. The statutory conclusive-presumption wording is not used to tell a defendant that knowledge or guilt is automatic.

## Proposed choices

### Alcohol DUI causing injury: VEH 23153(a)

Driving under the influence of alcohol, while concurrently committing an unlawful act or neglecting a legal driving duty that proximately causes bodily injury to someone other than the driver. Impairment or the charged blood-alcohol level alone is insufficient without the additional act/duty breach and causal injury.

Misdemeanor or felony. Ordinary first-offense punishment under section 23554 is 90 days to 364 days in county jail or 16 months, 2 years or 3 years in state prison, AND a $390-$1,000 base fine. First-offense probation under 23556 instead requires at least 5 days in jail, the fine and applicable programs. One qualifying prior offense within 10 years under 23560 raises the ordinary county-jail minimum to 120 days and the fine range to $390-$5,000; 23562 has separate probation/program alternatives, including a 30-day minimum under its specified program route. Two or more qualifying prior offenses within 10 years under 23566 ordinarily require 2, 3 or 4 years in state prison and a $1,015-$5,000 fine; 23568 governs available probation alternatives. Specified prior felony DUI or manslaughter convictions may independently invoke 23550.5. License suspension/revocation and separate injury or multiple-victim enhancements may apply; a generic prior-conviction answer is insufficient to assign a higher tier. Adult base penalties are shown; enhancements, assessments, restitution, probation and administrative licensing consequences require separate review.

Mental state: Voluntary driving with the charged impairment or blood-alcohol condition, concurrent unlawful conduct or neglected duty, and proximate injury causation.

### Driving a commercial vehicle with blood alcohol of 0.04 percent or more: VEH 23152(d)

Driving a commercial motor vehicle as defined in section 15210 with blood alcohol of 0.04 percent or more. A qualifying test within three hours creates a rebuttable presumption. The statutory commercial-vehicle definition controls; a vehicle is not commercial solely because driving is work-related.

Ordinarily a misdemeanor. First offense under 23536: 96 hours to 6 months in county jail AND a $390-$1,000 base fine; first-offense probation under 23538 has distinct program and custody conditions. A qualifying prior within 10 years can require 90 days to 364 days in jail under 23540, and two qualifying priors can require 120 days to 364 days under 23546, with the $390-$1,000 fine. Sections 23542 and 23548 govern their separate probation/program alternatives. Three or more qualifying priors within 10 years under 23550 can permit 16 months, 2 years or 3 years in state prison or 180 days to 364 days in county jail, plus the fine. Specified prior felony DUI or manslaughter convictions can independently invoke 23550.5, which permits state prison or up to 364 days in jail plus the fine. Suspension/revocation, treatment and interlock requirements depend on the case; no generic prior-conviction answer establishes a felony branch. Adult base penalties are shown; enhancements, assessments, restitution, probation and administrative licensing consequences require separate review.

Mental state: Voluntary driving with the charged blood-alcohol level and commercial-vehicle or actual paying-passenger condition.
