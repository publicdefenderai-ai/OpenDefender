# Texas protective-order firearm possession: bounded finite-term choices

## Publication scope

Five choices distinguish the order-authorizing categories listed in Penal Code §46.04(c). Every displayed name explicitly limits this publication to finite-term orders; the Chapter 7B choice is additionally limited to post-hearing orders. This is a partial treatment of the formerly deferred subsection, not a declaration that every protective order is valid or every constitutional application resolved. ## Statutory and constitutional boundaries

Notice must actually be received, the order must still be operative, and voluntary possession and the applicable culpable mental state remain necessary. A person's description as a respondent does not establish these conditions. The qualifying sworn, full-time paid peace-officer employment exclusion is retained, including the legal officer definition; the existing narrower officer selector elsewhere is not used to restrict this exclusion.

Finite duration and exclusion of ex-parte-only orders are publication limits, not invented elements of §46.04(c). Lifetime, indefinite, ex-parte-only and otherwise unsupported order applications remain outside these choices. The summaries expressly state that those exclusions do not authorize disregarding an order, and that finite duration alone does not establish constitutional validity. No prior owner decision or other existing hold is changed.

The retained official *Rahimi* opinion supplies its temporary credible-threat setting, not a rule that every state protective order is constitutional. The retained official *Noyes* per-curiam opinion (Texas Supreme Court No. 24-0023, May 15, 2026) vacates and remands the appellate judgment for further proceedings after *Rahimi*. The separate concurrences are not represented as the court's holding. The packet does not decide any individual order's constitutional validity, presume an expired order or import a federal criminal offense into the state statute.

The marriage-dissolution branch requires the specific §6.504 authority and separate protective-order document, not merely a divorce injunction. Chapter 85 summaries name §85.025(a-2) divorce-decree, (a-3) parent-child-final-order and (a-4) criminal-final-disposition duration triggers with their qualifying conditions. Those periods can extend beyond two years after issuance. The retained S.B. 1120 §§8,11,12 support the 2025 changes and rendition-date applicability; earlier orders require their then-applicable law. Article 7B.007 remains controlling for the Chapter 7B choice. Chapter 85 agreed/default conditions and statutory longer-term/confinement extensions are retained; private permission or filing a review motion does not itself terminate an order. The emergency-order summary uses current article 17.292(j)'s 61–91 and 91–121-day periods and instructs users to verify the actual order and earlier law for earlier issuance. The order may be effective on issuance, but §46.04(c) separately requires receipt of notice.

The Chapter 7B branch requires the actual post-hearing order and distinguishes statutory reasonable grounds from a constitutional credible-threat determination. The foreign-order branch preserves jurisdiction, due process, continued effectiveness, mutual-order conditions and the statutory defense in an enforcement action. Texas registration is not a prerequisite. The packet does not purport to retain every foreign jurisdiction's law or determine that an unknown foreign order qualifies.

Class A punishment is individually bound to §12.21. Section 46.11's knowledge/location enhancement and applicable prior-conviction rules are preserved without presuming their facts. Warnings about ammunition in other provisions do not enlarge this firearm offense into an ammunition-possession charge.
