## Legal boundaries

Indecency contact is second degree; the two exposure branches are third degree. Sexual intent, the actual conduct and the actor-exposure knowledge-of-presence element remain distinct. The text retains the statutory spouse and close-in-age defense conditions rather than treating consent or age proximity as a blanket defense. The registration-status alternatives use the statute's OR; they are not changed into a requirement to satisfy both negatives. The defense language is reported as statutory conditions, without deciding an individual constitutional challenge.

The five child sexual-assault alternatives remain separate. Their ordinary second-degree profile does not erase the prohibited-relationship first-degree branch or the separate aggravated offense. Section 22.011's close-in-age defense requires the victim to be at least 14 and its other conditions. Section 22.021 imports only the limited medical-care defense from 22.011(d), not the spouse/close-in-age defenses in (e).

Each of the five aggravated-child conduct alternatives has four age/aggravation profiles: ages 10 to 13 without an additional (a)(2)(A) circumstance; younger than 10; younger than 14 with that additional circumstance; and ages 14 to 16 with that additional circumstance. The 25-year minimum is assigned only to the two qualifying profiles. The generic first-degree range does not replace it. Fear that someone will become a listed trafficking victim is kept separate from the imminent death/serious-injury/kidnapping alternative. The distinct presence, episode, concerted-conduct, weapon and facilitating-substance findings are not assumed from age alone.

The existing whole-statute Sexual Assault and Aggravated Sexual Assault IDs remain whole-statute identities with corrected qualified penalty descriptions. Sexual Assault acknowledges the prohibited-relationship first-degree and assisted-reproduction state-jail branches. Aggravated Sexual Assault expressly distinguishes the 25-year minimum. The existing child(A) ID now displays a source-derived description of its exact conduct; it is not reassigned to a different act. Old static citations and generic defense boilerplate no longer override these reviewed records.

Chapter 62 reportable-conviction and registration provisions and §3.03 consecutive-sentencing authority are retained. Special repeat punishment under §12.42 requires its own exact current/prior/age findings. The ordinary-range choices do not establish a particular repeat predicate or clear a capital-punishment theory. No capital-sentence choice is introduced. Existing unrelated holds, including Chapter 51 and firearm protective-order branches, remain unchanged.
