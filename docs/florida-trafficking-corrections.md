# Florida trafficking corrections

Seventeen existing Florida charge choices cover 54 weight tiers under §893.135. Each choice keeps its statutory identity and subdivision. English, Spanish and Chinese guidance now names the covered acts, distinguishes the substance intended from the substance involved, and explains the rule for weighing qualifying mixtures. Every tier records its exact source passage, lower bound, exclusive upper bound where applicable, units, mandatory minimum term and fine. The three languages use the same numeric values.

The review corrected several differences between the prior summaries and the statute:

- In §893.135(1)(f)1, the precursor clause requires the listed precursors in conjunction with other chemicals and equipment used in manufacture. The former summary omitted that qualification.
- For hydrocodone and oxycodone the offense clause specifies a lower threshold. The last ordinary weight tier ends below 30 kg. Section (1)(c)5 addresses the named opioids at 30 kg or more. The guidance distinguishes the offense wording, tier and separate life-imprisonment branch. Cocaine and flunitrazepam have their own separately cited higher-weight branches.
- For fentanyl the base conduct appears in (1)(c)4.a., the ordinary fine and minimum in 4.b., and the conditional sale or delivery to a minor in 4.c. That enhancement also requires the specified product or packaging condition. Three additional identities have penalties in a sibling grading subdivision.
- For xylazine, chapter 2026-148 supplies the October 1, 2026 commencement. The §893.03 exception depends on an approved animal drug product and qualifying use. The statute expressly excludes the listed human-use activities from that exception.

The packet binds the restrictions on release, the prosecutor's discretionary substantial-assistance motion, and conditional domestic-repeat reclassification. A trafficking charge by itself establishes neither the required physical injury or death to a family or household member nor a prior domestic-violence conviction.
