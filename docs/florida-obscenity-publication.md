# Florida Chapter 847 publication review

The 2026 Florida Statutes support eight additional choices. Each record has its own conduct and grading references, English, Spanish and Chinese guidance, and an acquisition-bound quarterly verification window. Production selection also requires current database eligibility.

| Charge | Conduct | Grading | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Distribution of obscene material depicting minors | §847.011(1)(a) | §847.011(1)(c) | Distribution, offers and possession with intent to distribute. The depicted minor must be engaged in conduct harmful to minors. |
| Providing harmful visual images for payment | §847.012(3)(a) | §847.012(6) | Applies to listed visual depictions supplied to a minor for money. |
| Providing harmful publications or recordings for payment | §847.012(3)(b) | §847.012(6) | Separate written and audio branch, with its own content requirements. |
| Using a minor in production | §847.012(4) | §847.012(6) | Production can qualify regardless of intended or actual distribution to minors. |
| Distribution or posting on school property | §847.012(5) | §847.012(6) | Adult actor. The statute expressly excepts specified school-approved instructional materials. |
| Providing obscene material to a minor | §847.0133(1)–(2) | §847.0133(3) | Uses the obscenity standard and incorporates §847.012(1)'s knowledge definition. |
| Direct electronic transmission | §847.0138(1)–(2) | §847.0138(2) | Known or believed harmful content and a particular recipient known or believed to be under 18. |
| Direct electronic transmission from outside Florida | §847.0138(1), (3) | §847.0138(3) | Express out-of-state sender branch, with the same particular-recipient requirement. |

All eight carry an ordinary third-degree felony grade. The packet binds the distinct §847.001(7) and (12) definitions. Breastfeeding is expressly excluded under both definitions. Section 943.0435 lists §§847.0133 and 847.0138 among registration predicates. Its additional eligibility requirements matter. The school and materials sections have provisions concerning service providers in the publisher's session-law notes. The evidence retains those notes. General private possession under §847.011(2), including its constitutional limits, remains outside this batch.

In *Simmons v. State*, 944 So. 2d 317, 329 (Fla. 2006), the Florida Supreme Court read electronic mail to include individually addressed instant messages. The court reviewed an earlier version requiring knowledge or belief that the minor was in Florida. Chapter 2009-194 §8 removed the geographic language from the definition. The catalog avoids extrapolating jurisdiction to every transmission anywhere.
