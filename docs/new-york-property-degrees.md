## Boundaries that affect meaning

The monetary tests are strict **exceeds** thresholds ($250, $1,000, $3,000, $50,000 and the vehicle branch's $100), except religious-worship property uses **at least $100** and its separate qualifying-use/location/corporation predicates. The 155.30(9) row expressly retains an additional dependency hold: RCO 2 does not by itself establish incorporation or qualification under the Religious Corporations Law or Education Law. The corresponding incorporation provisions still need review.

Section 145.05(1) requires a locked vehicle, stealing intent, three or more specified convictions within ten years, separate criminal transactions and sentencing on separate occasions. Section 155.30's qualitative property branches do not inherit the $1,000 threshold. The vehicle branch separately excludes motorcycles. Value comes from 155.20, including its market-value/replacement hierarchy, instrument rules and unascertainable-value rule.

Section 155.40(2)(a) says physical injury, not serious physical injury; (b) concerns property damage; (c) requires the described misuse of public office. Each retains the common fear/actor-or-another condition. The limited defenses in 155.15 and the labor-group exception in 155.05 are retained, without describing them as universal defenses. Section 155.05's false-promise proof restriction also remains in the bound source; no broader common-law theory is inferred here.

The 155.30(12) aggregation alternative requires its common scheme/plan or single ongoing intent. The accompanying 70.10(1)(b)(iv) exception concerns **qualifying previous felony convictions**; it is not a claim that any person currently charged under this branch is immune from enhanced sentencing.

These are ordinary adult **nonviolent** C/D/E profiles, not the violent C framework used by second-degree burglary/robbery. Under 70.00, the indeterminate **maximum** is 3–15, 3–7 or 3–4 years respectively; the minimum period is at least one year and no more than one-third of the imposed maximum. A three-year maximum floor is not three years of mandatory confinement. The conditional definite-term alternative in 70.00(4) applies to D/E, not C. Individual fines are subject to 80.00's greater-of-$5,000-or-twice-gain rule. Youth, corporate, multiple-offender and special sentencing require separate treatment. Publisher-labelled pre-September-2027 authorization is bound separately from scheduled text.

## Duplicate subdivision labels and chronology

Both versions of subdivision 3 in each of 155.35 and 155.40 remain in the official source corpus with unique candidate IDs and exact offsets: retail aggregation and deed theft. No version is declared invalid, superseded or repealed merely because its number is duplicated. These four alternatives are held for separate enactment/applicability and dependency review; that hold does not erase the other mapped alternatives.

A bounded official-document check identified the separate enactment paths:

- [2024 enacted A8805-C, Part FF](https://assembly.state.ny.us/2024budget/bills2024/enacted/A8805c.pdf), printed pages 79–81, adds the retail-aggregation branches and the prior-conviction exception. Part FF section 6 specifies a ninetieth-day commencement rule.
- [2024 enacted A8806-C, Part O](https://assembly.state.ny.us/2024budget/bills2024/enacted/A8806c.pdf), printed pages 39–49, adds the deed-theft provisions; section 13 has a ninetieth-day commencement rule.
- The official [155.35(3) deed-theft CJI](https://www.nycourts.gov/judges/cji/2-PenalLaw/155/155.35%283%29.Dead_Theft.pdf) identifies conduct on or after July 19, 2024 and a deed-theft-specific theory.

These linked documents are follow-up sources inspected through the browser, not additional hash-bound documents in the 27-source packet. Their identification is not a completed chronology or judicial-limitation review. No effectiveDateStart is inferred from a publisher revision date. Deed-theft definitions, applicable enactment history and instruction limitations must be fully bound before promoting those alternatives.
