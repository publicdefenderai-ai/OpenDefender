## Important distinctions

Child-passenger DWI is ordinarily a state jail felony, not the ordinary Class B DWI offense. Section 49.09(b) separately supplies third-degree punishment for one qualifying intoxication-manslaughter conviction or two qualifying intoxication convictions. Prior-conviction definitions, finality and double-use restrictions are retained; the candidate does not determine that an unspecified historical or out-of-state prior qualifies. A passenger aged fifteen does not satisfy the younger-than-fifteen requirement. Injury and parenthood are not added elements.

Intoxication assault requires the offense-specific serious-bodily-injury definition and the causal connection to intoxication. Ordinary bodily injury or intoxication coincident with a crash is not enough. The traumatic-brain-injury enhancement requires a persistent vegetative state; coma or concussion is not silently substituted. Firefighter/EMS actual-duty injury is second degree; qualifying peace-officer/judge actual-duty injury is first degree. The officer selector is limited to the four roles common to the retained article 2A.001 versions, while the explanation preserves the legally applicable grade for other qualifying officers. It does not import the knowledge-of-status or retaliation alternatives of unrelated assault statutes.

The public-place requirement attaches to motor-vehicle operation, not automatically to aircraft, watercraft, rides or mobile-ride assembly. No separate culpable mental state is required by §49.11, but the conduct, intoxication and injury/death causation elements remain. Lawful substance use is not itself a defense.

## Two 2025 manslaughter amendments

Both official §49.09(b-2) texts are retained without deleting or rewriting either. They share the protected-victim enhancement. S.B. 745 additionally supplies a first-degree multiple-deaths/same-criminal-transaction branch from September 1, 2025; H.B. 2017 instead additionally addresses a qualifying prior intoxication conviction together with a Chapter 51 violation.

The Chapter 51-dependent branch is not cleared in this batch. Its predicate, applicable version and enforcement posture require separate verification. This is not a declaration that Chapter 51 is invalid, unenforceable or presently enjoined, and the candidate does not infer that predicate from a person's immigration status. The general manslaughter explanation discloses this limitation rather than promising second-degree treatment for every case. The multiple-death choice identifies its particular enacted-text version and does not erase the other amendment.

## Sentencing and instructions

Article 42A.401 requires minimum confinement when community supervision is granted: thirty days for §49.07, 120 days for §49.08, and ten days for the §49.09(b)-enhanced child-passenger branches. This does not promise eligibility for supervision. For applicable offenses from September 1, 2023, article 42.0375 requires court-set monthly support restitution for the child of the manslaughter victim, with its age/high-school terms and prohibition on requiring payments to a recipient aged nineteen or older. The statutory arrears rule is not omitted. Section 3.03(b)(1) permits, but does not invariably require, consecutive injury/death sentences. Transportation Code §709.001's additional fine and indigency waiver are limited to qualifying motor-vehicle convictions.

The publisher's edition 055 CPJC 49.17, 49.25 and 49.26 pages were checked directly. Their links are explicitly limited to motor-vehicle/public-place underlying conduct, with special grading separate. The pages do not certify the new 2025 enhancements, officer classification or nonmotor alternatives. No copyrighted instruction body is reproduced. New English-only content uses the explicit non-English fallback notice.
