## Scope and boundaries

| Section | Mapped alternatives | Grade | Remaining scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| 140.05 | Whole offense | Violation | Judicial construction of unlawful entry/license and conduct-date review remain |
| 140.15 | (1), (2) | A misdemeanor | Registration/risk status and school exceptions must be applied independently |
| 215.50 | (1)–(4) | A misdemeanor | (5), (6), (7) separately held |
| 240.26 | (1)–(3) | Violation | Speech, legitimate-purpose and labor-activity boundaries remain |

Each row binds its own conduct, the shared statutory preamble where applicable, its section's grading text, sentencing and fine spans, and the exact dependency set. The ten mappings are alternatives, not ten existing catalog identities or ten new live choices.

- Trespass under 140.05 is a violation. The 140.00 premises, dwelling and entry/license definitions are retained; public access is not automatically unlawful entry.
- Section 140.15(2) requires Article 6-C registration and level two or three designation, plus knowledge that the registration-offense victim attends **or formerly attended** the school. All five exceptions remain bound. The separate dwelling/unlawful-entry wording of (1) is not transplanted into (2). Correction Law 168-A, 168-F and 168-L provide retained registration and classification context, not a finding that any particular person is registered or barred from a school. Other Article 6-C duration, relief and individual applicability provisions remain a publication prerequisite if needed for product claims.
- Contempt (1) requires a sitting court, immediate view/presence and the specified interference/respect tendency. Paragraph (2) requires a direct tendency to interrupt proceedings. Neither mapping clears mere criticism or constitutionally protected expression. Judicial speech, notice and procedural limits remain unresolved for product publication.
- Contempt (3) retains **intentional** disobedience/resistance to **lawful** process/mandate and the Judiciary Law 753-a(2) labor-dispute exclusion. Paragraph (4) retains contumacious, **unlawful** refusal and **legal and proper** questioning; privilege and immunity cannot be ignored.
- Contempt (5) and (7) are held for constitutional/judicial review of publication and courthouse expression. Paragraph (6) is held because incorporated jury statutes, rules and special statutes have not been fully bound. These are review holds, not assertions that statutory text is invalid.
- Harassment retains intent to harass, annoy or alarm in all three branches. The course/repeated-acts branch separately requires alarming/seriously annoying acts and no legitimate purpose. The named federal labor-law exclusion applies to (2) and (3), not (1); this packet does not decide whether particular conduct is federally regulated.
- Threats in 240.26(1) are not equated with offensive speech. [People v Lagano, 2022 NY Slip Op 07021](https://www.nycourts.gov/Reporter/3dseries/2022/2022_07021.htm) discusses genuine threats and constitutional limits under *Dietze*. This linked judicial reference is not counted as a retained, hash-bound source; complete judicial/CJI and offense-date review remains required.

## Ordinary sentencing only

If imprisonment is imposed, the ordinary A-misdemeanor maximum is **364 days**, not one year; the ordinary violation maximum is **15 days**. Ordinary individual fines are **$1,000** and **$250**, respectively. Section 80.05(5) separately permits an alternative fine up to twice gain, incorporating 80.00(2)–(3). Corporate fines, restitution, fees, individual eligibility and special dispositions are not resolved by these summaries. Section 60.01 and 65.00 context is retained; no sentence or alternative disposition is guaranteed.
