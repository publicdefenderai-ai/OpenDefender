# Florida child sexual-offense corrections

Eight existing Florida charges now distinguish the ordinary penalty from consequences that require additional proof. These are four records under §800.04(4)–(7), three under §827.071(2)(a), (2)(b) and (3), and the already configured impregnation offense under §827.04(3).

| Scope | What changed |
| --- | --- |
| Lewd or lascivious battery and molestation | The guidance differentiates offender and victim ages, qualifying prior convictions and the special §775.082 sentence for an adult convicted of molestation of a child under 12. The repeat-offender minimum in §794.0116 applies to molestation. |
| Lewd or lascivious conduct and exhibition | These records retain their offender-age grading and receive the corresponding ordinary ranges. They are outside the §794.0115 and §794.0116 lists used here. |
| Use, aggravated use and promotion of a child in a performance | The primary adult minimums are 15, 25 and five years respectively. Only the first and third branches reference the four written findings permitting departure. A qualifying prior under §794.0116 brings separate minimums of 30 years for use/aggravated use and 20 years for promotion. Section 775.0847 lists a grade increase for the second-degree promotion branch when the image conditions are met. |
| Impregnation | The existing offense requires an offender aged at least 21 and a victim under 16. This batch adds ordinary penalties and the separately conditioned domestic-violence rule. The hold on §827.04(1) continues. |

The five applicable offenses under §794.0115 carry a separate 50-year minimum for conduct on or after October 1, 2014 only when the adult-offender requirement and a listed aggravating circumstance are met. The four §800.04 and three §827.071 records also distinguish registration eligibility from the sentencing decision. The under-12 aggravated-use record does not offer the age-proximity removal route.

The packet retains 15 official Florida sections, with ten acquired for this batch and five reused. Chapters 2026-18 and 2026-20 took effect July 1, 2026. The former changes terminology while the latter changes sentencing provisions. Chapter 2026-86 supplies the separate domestic-violence effective date. Each record keeps the earlier translated summaries and its charge identity. No new charge, selection, receipt or import is implied.

Including the property and fraud batch on the parent branch, 75 distinct records in the older Florida family of 101 have received targeted corrections; 26 remain outside those batches. The existing receipt cannot authorize the corrected aggregate. No statewide denominator has been established.
