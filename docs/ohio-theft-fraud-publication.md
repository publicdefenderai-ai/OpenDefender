## Material distinctions preserved

- Vehicle-use (A) can be M1; the out-of-state/more-than-48-hours (B) branch starts at F5. Loss to an elderly/disabled victim invokes a different ladder and the reasonable-belief defenses are retained.
- Organized retail theft begins at $7,500, aggregates over twelve months, and uses F3/F2/F1 thresholds with express prior-conviction prison rules. Mail theft uses its own F5 base and incorporated theft-value provisions.
- Telecommunications fraud retains both the value ladder and protected-victim F4 clause. The published explanation does not invent a resolution of their interaction or promise an F4 ceiling.
- Counterfeiting identifies the mandatory $500 minimum fine in its default F4 clause and the five-device alternative. It does not flatten overlapping count/value provisions into an unconditional lower grade or infer the fine's application to every branch.
- Trademark manufacture, production tools, and sale/trafficking use different grade rules. Unit-count thresholds remain alternatives to price, exactly 100 is not more than 100, and forfeiture/defenses are stated.
- Government records and unrevoked wills receive their own tampering grades. Protected-victim securing-writings thresholds and the elderly-victim additional $50,000 fine are retained.
- Workers' compensation fraud distinguishes fraudulent intent from mere lack of coverage and identifies additional investigation/prosecution costs.

The remaining benefit-fraud and spamming provisions, general theft branch expansion, and other unscheduled Chapter 2913 sections are outside this batch. Existing configured generic records are not represented as complete subdivision coverage.
