The financial felony choices address the benchmark's failure-to-report scenario. Section 655.50(5)(d) ties reporting to required records. Subsection (5)(a) has a separate business-day rule for aggregating cash in or cash out. The 12-month amounts in (10)(b) grade a proven violation. They cannot establish the underlying reporting duty. Under (5)(e), a timely qualifying federal filing counts subject to the statutory federal-transmission exception. Federal offenses, filing instructions, administrative penalties and civil penalties have no separate criminal choices here.

Section 655.50(10)(c) authorizes an additional discretionary criminal fine on conviction or a guilty or no-contest plea. The maximum is the greater of $250,000 or twice the transaction value, or the greater of $500,000 or five times the value for a subsequent qualifying conviction or plea. The ordinary imprisonment descriptions concern natural persons.

The Legislature’s current 2026 text incorporates both the June 26 amendment and the ratified July 1 amendment to the financial definitions. The publisher identifies those dates in the note to §655.50(3)(e). Chapter 2026-130 §11 separately reenacted the reporting clause. These dates describe particular amendments.
