Nineteen exact choices cover five §22.05 scopes and seven §22.11 facility/service-nexus scopes, each divided into listed bodily substances and the added other-fluid/liquid predicate. ## Statutory boundaries

- Recklessness and imminent danger of serious bodily injury remain necessary for §22.05(a). No firearm or actual injury is required. The firearm-pointing presumption does not establish actual discharge for (b).
- Section 22.05(b)(2) requires conscious disregard of occupancy risk, not proof of actual occupancy; each property type uses §30.01. Subsection (b)(1) has its own individual-target predicate.
- S.B. 1637, effective September 1, 2025, excludes lawfully acting peace officers from (c)’s presumption and adds (f)’s separate narrow (b)(1) exception requiring actual official duty plus reasonable belief in Chapter 9 justification. Neither rule is blanket officer immunity or an automatic (b)(2) exception. Its any-element transition is retained.
- Section 22.06’s consent defense is limited to its no-serious-injury or specified occupation/medical/scientific-risk conditions, with the criminal-street-gang exclusion. Chapter 9 justification retains its own conditions.
- Section 22.11 requires the specific intent and actual contact. The confined-actor branch, TCCO duty and retaliation branches, contractor duty and retaliation branches, and known-public-servant duty and retaliation branches are not collapsed. Retaliation does not silently acquire a contemporaneous-duty requirement. The civil-facility definition is §1.07(a)(8-a).
- Subsections (e)/(f) create distinct knowledge presumptions. Section 2.05(a) retains the predicate-proof and permissive-inference safeguards; status does not prove intent, contact or the applicable duty/retaliation nexus.
- The 2017 enacted text already covered the six listed bodily substances from humans OR animals. S.B. 1610 did not first add animal substances in 2025. It added other fluids/liquids, additional intent alternatives, the civil-facility uniform presumption and the warning rule. Those changes are subject to its September 1, 2025 any-element transition and are not backdated.

The H.B. 2017/Chapter 51 intoxication predicate remains separately unresolved. This batch neither clears it nor makes a claim about current enforcement posture. Existing speech and other source holds remain unchanged.
