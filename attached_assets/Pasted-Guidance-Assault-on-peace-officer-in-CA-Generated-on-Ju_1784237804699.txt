Being arrested and detained on an assault on a peace officer charge in California is a serious situation that moves quickly. Under California law, arraignment must happen within 48 hours of arrest, which is when bail will be set and formal charges will be read. The most important things right now are securing legal representation before that arraignment, understanding bail options, and being aware that this charge is a felony that carries significant consequences. The key issues that typically determine how these cases go include the circumstances of the alleged contact with the officer, any available video footage, and whether the person has any prior criminal history.

What Matters Now
0%

Legal Representation: Having an attorney before arraignment matters because bail arguments are made at that first hearing. For someone detained on an assault on a peace officer charge, the difference between being represented and unrepresented at arraignment can affect whether bail is set, and at what amount. If you cannot afford an attorney, a public defender will be appointed, but contacting the public defender's office as early as possible gives them more time to prepare.
urgent

Phone Call Right: California law (Cal. Penal Code 851.5) requires law enforcement to provide access to a phone within 3 hours of arrest. This right exists specifically so a person can contact an attorney or arrange for one. If this access has not been provided, that is something to raise with an attorney immediately.
urgent

Bail and OR Release: At arraignment, the judge will decide whether to set bail, release on own recognizance (OR release, meaning no money required), or hold without bail. Assault on a peace officer is a felony, and bail amounts in these cases can be substantial. An attorney can argue for lower bail or OR release based on ties to the community, employment, and other factors.
urgent

Right to Remain Silent: In California, as everywhere in the U.S., a person has the right to remain silent and to have an attorney present before any questioning about the facts of the case. Whether or how to respond to law enforcement questions about the incident is a decision that attorneys commonly advise clients to make only after consulting with counsel.

Your Rights
•
In California, a person charged with a felony has the right to a preliminary hearing, where a judge decides whether there is enough evidence for the case to proceed to trial. This typically happens within 10 court days of arraignment if the person is in custody.
•
California law provides the right to a jury trial for felony charges. The jury must reach a unanimous verdict to convict.
•
A person in custody has the right to reasonable bail unless the charge is one for which bail can be denied. Assault on a peace officer is generally bailable, though the amount can be high.
•
If the alleged assault involved a disputed account of what happened, the defense has the right to challenge the prosecution's evidence, cross-examine witnesses including the officer, and present its own evidence at trial.
•
California Penal Code 851.5 gives arrested persons the right to make at least three completed phone calls within 3 hours of arrest, at no charge for local calls.

Important Warnings
•
Assault on a peace officer is treated more seriously than a standard assault charge in California. Prosecutors and courts typically take these cases seriously, and penalties upon conviction can include jail or prison time, fines, and a felony record.
•
Any statements made to law enforcement about the incident can be used in court. Attorneys commonly caution that even statements that seem to explain or justify what happened can be used against a person. This is a topic to discuss only with an attorney.
•
If a protective or no-contact order is issued as a condition of bail or release, violating it can result in immediate re-arrest and loss of bail. These orders sometimes arise in cases involving officers or witnesses.
•
A felony conviction in California can affect professional licenses, certain types of employment, and firearm rights. These are called collateral consequences and are separate from any criminal sentence. An attorney can help identify which consequences apply.
Court Preparation
•
**Arraignment Appearance**: At arraignment, the judge will read the charges and ask for a plea. Attorneys commonly advise clients not to enter a plea of guilty at arraignment without having reviewed the evidence. A plea of not guilty at this stage preserves all options.
•
**Dress and Conduct**: Courts generally respond better to defendants who appear respectful and composed. Dressing neatly and speaking only when addressed by the judge is standard guidance for any court appearance.
•
**Bring Documentation**: If released before a future court date, having documentation of employment, family ties, and community connections can be useful for bail arguments or future hearings. An attorney can advise on what is relevant.
•
**Know Your Court Date**: Missing any court date in California results in a bench warrant for arrest and potential bail forfeiture. Keeping track of every scheduled date is essential.

Employment & Licensing
Upon conviction
A felony conviction for assault on a peace officer can disqualify a person from jobs requiring a background check, security clearances, or positions working with vulnerable populations. California's ban-the-box law delays when employers can ask about criminal history, but does not prevent consideration after a conditional offer.

Ask your attorney about the specific employment categories affected and whether any diversion or reduced charge options could limit this consequence. See [Employment Rights](/support/employment) for more information.

Firearms Rights
Upon conviction
A felony conviction in California results in a lifetime prohibition on owning or possessing firearms under both state and federal law.

Raise this consequence with your attorney when discussing all possible case outcomes, including plea options.

Child Custody
Pending charge can be raised immediately; conviction has longer-term impact
A felony conviction, or even a pending felony charge, can be raised in family court proceedings related to child custody or visitation. Courts consider criminal history as part of the best interest of the child analysis.

If there are active custody proceedings, inform your criminal defense attorney so they can coordinate with any family law attorney involved. See [Family Care](/support/family-care) for support resources.

Other Consequence
Upon conviction, at license renewal or application
Certain professional licenses in California (healthcare, law, teaching, contracting) may be affected by a felony conviction. The state requires a direct relationship between the conviction and the licensed activity before a board can deny or revoke a license, but assault on a peace officer may meet that standard for some professions.

If you hold or are pursuing a professional license, raise this with your attorney early. The Collateral Consequences Resource Center at ccrcatlaw.org has state-specific information on which licenses are affected.

Common Concerns
•
Assault on a peace officer cases often involve body camera footage, witness officers, and official reports. Attorneys commonly note that the official account in these cases can be detailed and consistent, which makes having independent evidence and legal representation especially important early on.
•
Social media posts about the incident or the arrest can be seen by prosecutors and may be used in court. This concern is specific to cases involving law enforcement, where prosecutors may actively look for statements that contradict a defense.
•
In cases involving law enforcement, contact with any witnesses, including other officers or bystanders, before speaking with an attorney raises serious concerns about witness contact issues. Attorneys advise taking no action regarding witnesses until counsel has been consulted.
•
If there are any conditions attached to release on bail or OR, such as check-ins or geographic restrictions, violating those conditions in a felony case can result in immediate re-arrest and the court viewing the case more seriously.
Areas of Uncertainty
4
Bail amount for assault on a peace officer in this county

Bail schedules vary by county in California. The specific bail amount for this charge depends on the county where the arrest occurred, the specific sub-charge (misdemeanor vs. felony, injury vs. no injury), and the judge's discretion. Confirm with your attorney or public defender.

Whether the charge is filed as a misdemeanor or felony

Assault on a peace officer can be charged as a misdemeanor (Penal Code 241(c)) or felony (Penal Code 243(c)) depending on whether injury occurred and other facts. The specific filing decision is made by the prosecutor and may not be final at the arrest stage.

Weekend and holiday impact on 48-hour arraignment deadline

California courts have interpreted the 48-hour rule to exclude weekends and holidays in some circumstances. The exact arraignment date may shift depending on when the arrest occurred. Confirm the scheduled arraignment date with the jail or an attorney.
