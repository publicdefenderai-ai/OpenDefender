Priority 1: Accuracy — Must Do Before Launch
Task #157 — Close i18n translation gaps
The English locale file (en.ts) is 120 lines longer than the Spanish and Chinese versions. That's ~120 missing keys that silently fall back to English for Spanish- and Chinese-speaking users. Worse, the immigration document checklist has no Chinese support at all — the data structure doesn't even have a zh field. For a site explicitly serving Spanish and Chinese speakers, this is a first-priority fix.
Task #158 — External link audit
The diversion programs were last verified April 10, 2026. The support pages collectively have 65 external links to nonprofits, government programs, and crisis hotlines. Three months is a long time for government and nonprofit URLs to move. A broken hotline number for someone in crisis is a serious failure. The diversion programs checker script already exists (scripts/check-diversion-programs.ts) — it just needs to be run, and any dead links fixed.
Task #162 — Legal disclaimer and privacy policy review
Before you announce publicly, both the /disclaimers and /privacy-policy pages need a read-through to confirm they accurately reflect the current feature set. The site has grown significantly — the AI narrative, the document summarizer, the public API, the advocate toolkit — and the disclaimers should cover all of it. The effective date needs to be current.

Priority 2: Ease of Use & Accessibility — Should Do Before Launch
Task #159 — Fix keyboard navigation and aria-labels
The header navigation dropdowns open on mouse hover but have no keyboard focus handlers. A user navigating by keyboard (or a screen reader) cannot open the main nav menus. This is a fundamental WCAG 2.1 failure. Additionally, several buttons in the immigration section and the charge selector lack aria-label attributes, making them opaque to screen readers. Given that a significant portion of your users may be in crisis, in custody, or using assistive technology, this matters.

Priority 3: Substantive Utility — Nice to Have at Launch
Task #160 — Mitigation Builder print stylesheet
This is the one deferred feature worth shipping at launch. Public defenders use the mitigation memo in courtrooms, on paper, in front of judges. Right now the only export is .docx. Adding a clean @media print stylesheet and a "Print / Save as PDF" button costs very little and meaningfully expands the tool's usefulness for its core audience.
